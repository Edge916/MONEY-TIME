# QuotePage paid-pilot experiment

## Objective and buyer

Find whether independent mobile detailers will pay $249 for a small, finished website, and whether they separately value $49/month care. The first 14-day test costs $0 upfront. It uses free hosting, a free Gmail inbox, and current design tools. No paid advertising, domains, software subscriptions, or AI API credits.

Choose one geographic market after the owner identifies where they can reasonably sell. A qualifying business is active, independent, has recent public evidence of detailing work, and has a specific website/contact problem we can fix within one page. The owner can be reached through a legitimate business channel. Exclude closed businesses, franchises, sites already doing the job well, and projects requiring ecommerce or custom booking systems.

For every candidate, record the business name, source URL, date reviewed, exact observed issue, a possible next step, contact stage, and objections. A directory listing alone is not proof that the business lacks a website. Check its linked website and public profiles. Do not populate the tracker with fictional prospects.

## The offer

**$249 one-time build.** One responsive page, up to three service packages, service area, contact links, owner-approved photos, and one revision round. A free hosting subdomain is included in the launch approach. An optional custom domain is purchased directly by the customer. The buyer retains website files and host/domain access. Target delivery: five business days after receiving complete approved details, subject to an agreed start date.

**$49/month optional care.** One small content update per month, capped at 20 minutes, plus a monthly contact-link check. Month-to-month; cancel before the next renewal. Exclude new pages, redesigns, SEO campaigns, ads, booking platforms, and unlimited updates. The build purchase and care choice are separate.

Give a free generic or lightly personalized sample. Agree on the scope and price before building the full customer version. For the first pilot, use one $249 payment after the buyer approves the preview and before launch. Payment setup and seller details must be valid first. If using deposits or multiple payments later, update fees and terms; two transactions have two fixed processing charges. Track only collected payments as revenue.

## Draft sales page copy

**Headline:** Your work looks good. Your website can too.

**Body:** Give customers one clear place to see your services, understand your pricing, and request a quote. We handle the design and setup.

**Offer:** A one-page website for $249. Responsive design, up to three packages, service area, contact links, and one round of revisions. Start on a free host subdomain. Optional care is $49/month for one small update and a monthly contact-link check.

**Call to action:** See your business on a page.

**Expectations:** Review your services, prices, contact details, and approved photos before launch. Customer acquisition still depends on referrals, local visibility, and sharing your site. No bookings or search rankings are promised.

The functional private prototype contains this offer and a sample generator. The sample company and prices are illustrative. It currently collects no payments or inquiries. Its contact configuration awaits a verified business email.

## Conversation and message drafts

These are drafts for owner-authorized use. No messages have been sent.

Warm introduction: “I'm testing a simple website service for mobile detailers. The page shows services, pricing, service area, and a clear quote link. It's $249 to build, with optional care at $49/month. Do you know an operator who would find that useful?”

Individual approach: “Hi [owner], I looked at [verified business URL] and noticed [specific, accurate issue]. I'm testing a small website service for mobile detailers: one page with your services, service area, and quote/contact links, for $249. Optional care is $49/month. Would you like to see a sample using your business details?”

After they agree: ask how they currently receive inquiries, what frustrates customers, who updates prices/photos, which tool they already use, and whether the quoted price fits their budget. Ask what would need to be true for them to purchase. Listen before adding features.

One follow-up, where appropriate: “Following up on the sample I shared. Is a simple website something you want to fix now? If it isn't a priority, I'll leave it there.” Stop when asked and record the outcome. Adapt sender details and the message to the actual channel and location before use.

## Pass, change, and stop rules

After **20 qualified, actually contacted businesses** over up to 14 days:

- **Pass:** at least 5 substantive conversations and at least 1 paid $249 pilot. Time the pilot delivery. Continue cautiously if a buyer is willing to pay but payment setup is the only outstanding step; record it as commitment, not revenue.
- **Change:** conversations happen but nobody buys. Classify the top objections. Test one revised scope or customer segment for the next 10 qualified contacts. Do not simply build more features or buy ads.
- **Pause this offer:** fewer than 3 substantive conversations after 20 accurate, qualified contacts, or zero paid pilots after 40 qualified contacts and one controlled change. Move to interviews or test the original spreadsheet offer. No paid software build.
- **Delivery failure:** a pilot takes over 6 hours or needs unsupported functionality. Narrow the scope or revise pricing before selling another. Do not promise the same scope at an unsustainable price.
- **Care validation:** count an explicit $49 opt-in, a completed care task, and a paid renewal. A website sale alone does not validate recurring revenue. Rework care if buyers see no ongoing need.

These small samples produce directional evidence, not a statistically reliable market-size estimate. Track qualified contacts, replies, conversations, commitments, payments, care opt-ins, renewals, cancellations, support hours, and actual money retained. Measure each rate with its matching denominator.

## Launch inputs still needed

The assistant connects the inbox to the website, verifies the contact path, and customizes and checks customer designs during active work. Public access and actual outreach come in the launch task. The current preview is private and browser-local generation does not submit customer information.
